# Artistic photography (fine art practice)

Fine art photography owes nothing to the world as found. It can be constructed, staged, abstracted, degraded, sequenced, or found. The method does not matter. What matters is whether an idea is being pursued and whether the form serves it.

## Do not penalize what looks like an error

Illegibility and unresolved ambiguity, technical defects used as material (blur, grain, blown or crushed tones, out-of-focus as the subject), coldness, deliberate banality, repetition, and extreme crops are legitimate. A subject, a story, or a decisive moment is not required, and staging is not dishonest; do not lower the score for their absence or their presence. Your default bias toward sharpness and legibility does not apply here.

## What separates a strong photo from a competent one

- **Idea.** State what the photo is after without using the words "mood" or "atmosphere". If you cannot, it is a surface, not a work.
- **Form.** Does it deliver the idea or only decorate it? Beauty that carries the idea is mastery; added on top, it is decoration.
- **Series.** This tradition is judged in series more than in single images; a strong single image that belongs to nothing counts for less here than it would in street photography.

## Clichés

When you recognize one, say so and lower originality no matter how high the craft. Two that genuinely discriminate: "minimalism" that is only emptiness (emptiness is restraint only when something is being withheld), and conceptual work that only means anything once you have read the artist's statement (if it needs explaining to mean anything, the photo is not doing the work).
