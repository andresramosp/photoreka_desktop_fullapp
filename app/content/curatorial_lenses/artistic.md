# Artistic photography (fine art practice)

Fine art photography does not owe anything to the world as found. It can be constructed, staged, abstracted, degraded, sequenced, or found. The method does not matter. What matters is whether an idea is being pursued and whether the form serves it.

## Things that look like errors but are usually deliberate

- **Illegibility and unresolved ambiguity.** Not knowing what you are looking at is often the content. Do not treat clarity as an obligation.
- **Technical "defects" used as material.** Blur, grain, dust, scratches, flare, chemical damage, blown or crushed tones, out-of-focus as the subject.
- **No subject, no story, no decisive moment.** This tradition is not obliged to provide any of them. Do not penalize their absence.
- **Staging and construction.** A built image is not a dishonest one. Candidness is not a criterion here.
- **Coldness, flatness of affect, deliberate banality, repetition, series.**
- **Extreme crops; scale or orientation that cannot be resolved.**

## What separates a strong fine art photo from a competent one

- **Is an idea being pursued?** State what the photo is after without using the words "mood" or "atmosphere". If you cannot, it is a surface, not a work.
- **Is the frame non-arbitrary?** Would moving the camera ten centimeters destroy it? If not, no decision was made, and tonal beauty does not substitute for one.
- **Does form deliver the idea, or decorate it?** Beauty that carries the idea is mastery. Beauty added on top of it is decoration.
- **Does it survive a second look?** Productive ambiguity deepens on rereading. Vagueness evaporates.
- **Does it belong to a body of work?** This tradition is judged in series more than in single images. A strong single image that belongs to nothing counts for less here than it would in street photography.

## Clichés that score high on craft and low on originality

Say exactly that when you see one.

- Golden-hour and blue-hour spectacle; the lone tree; the misty forest; silky long-exposure water; the symmetrical dawn reflection; oversaturated skies; HDR landscapes.
- The pretty texture; bokeh light blobs; a saturated color field with no tension; the symmetrical architecture pattern; the drone grid; the long-exposure blurred crowd.
- "Minimalism" that is only emptiness. Emptiness is restraint only when something is being withheld.
- The moody window-light portrait with a vacant stare, presented as psychological depth.
- Conceptual work that only makes sense once you have read the artist's statement. If the photo needs explaining to mean anything, the photo is not doing the work.
- Fluent imitation of a well-known style without the reason behind it.
