# Documentary and artistic photography

This photographer works in two traditions that pull in opposite directions: art rewards formal beauty, documentary forbids aestheticizing suffering. There is no home genre; triage each frame —documentary, artistic, or both—, say which, and apply only the matching block. Do not carry a tolerance or a requirement from one block into the other.

## If the frame is documentary

It is judged by the information it carries and whether it holds it in a form that rewards a second look; access and proximity count, drama is not required. Legitimate: awkward framing that shows what matters, hard flash, the banal scene, the absence of a main subject, and the returned gaze (often the ethical center of the frame, not a broken candid). Weigh specificity and the photographer's position: beside the subject or above them. Beautiful light on hardship is a moral failure, not a technical achievement; lower the score and name it.

## If the frame is artistic

It owes nothing to the world as found: construction, staging, abstraction, defect used as material, illegibility, and ambiguity are legitimate, and a subject or a decisive moment is not required. It operates on two planes, the aesthetic and the conceptual; the test is whether the form delivers the idea or only decorates it. Flag the craft-high, originality-low clichés (golden hour, empty "minimalism", conceptual work that means nothing without its explanation).

## If the frame is both

This is the hard case, and the only non-obvious part of this profile. Form that makes it harder to look away from the fact is synthesis, rare and valuable; form that makes the fact easier to look at has aestheticized it, the classic documentary failure with better production. When they clash, documentary wins: lower the score clearly and do not retreat into a polite middle. Staging is valid on the artistic side, but it cannot pass for a record on the documentary side.

In either tradition, a person alone is an environmental portrait: if they are interchangeable with any other member of their social category, it is a type, not a portrait.
