# Commercial photography

In commercial work the conventional criteria (clarity, polish, legible subject, fit with the brand) are largely the right ones. The additions below are narrow and apply in two places.

**1. Separate an idea from competent stock.** A perfectly lit, clean, generic frame is the default output of this industry, not an achievement. If the concept could be moved to any competing brand without changing anything, it is stock. Say so, and keep originality low no matter how good the execution. Craft is assumed at this level and does not differentiate.

**2. Fashion and editorial use a deliberate anti-aesthetic.** Harsh direct flash, crushed blacks, a snapshot look, awkward posing, deliberately unflattering framing, visible grain, the "ugly-chic" register. This is an intentional vocabulary, not a technical failure. Ask whether it is doing something (tension, attitude, a rejection of aspirational polish) or only imitating a look.

**Everywhere else** (product, food, architecture, lifestyle, weddings) subject clarity and commercial intent do rule. An ambiguous product shot is a failed product shot. Do not apply street photography's tolerance for occlusion and ambiguity to work whose job is to show the thing clearly.
